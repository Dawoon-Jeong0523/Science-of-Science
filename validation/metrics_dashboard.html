Metrics

8 families · 3–4 windows each

Every metric is computed at four horizons — 3, 5, 10 years and the full history — so that a result can always be re-stated on a truncation-safe window. Papers and patents get the same families wherever the underlying graph allows it.

Citation counts

C3 C5 C10 Call

Forward citations inside a fixed window after publication or grant.

Papers, patents and patent→paper all carry the same four windows. Patents additionally split every window by examiner / non-examiner / unknown, carry pre-grant application citations (appC), and a de-duplicated variant (uniqueC) that counts each citing patent once.

Disruption

CD3 CD5 CD10 CDall

Funk & Owen-Smith CD index: does later work cite this instead of its references (disruptive, → +1) or alongside them (consolidating, → −1)?

Stored with its counting terms ni (citing the focal work only), nj (citing both) and nk (citing the references only), so any variant can be recomputed without touching the graph.

F / E / G

F E G

Foundation, Extension, Generalization — the three-way decomposition of the disruption index.

They sum to exactly 1 by construction, which §7 of the patent notebook uses as a build check.

Atypicality

Z_median Z_10pct Z_min n_pairs

Uzzi et al. journal-pair z-scores against a degree-preserving null. Median Z = conventionality, 10th-percentile Z = tail novelty.

Papers use journal pairs; patents use CPC pairs (Kim et al. 2016 style). The pairwise z-scores are kept in a separate z_score_pair table.

Sleeping Beauty

SB_B SB_T

Ke et al. beauty coefficient B and awakening time T from the full citation history.

B measures how far the real trajectory sits below the straight line to its peak; T is where that gap is widest.

Hit probability

pctl_c3 pctl_c5 pctl_c10 pctl_call

Percentile rank of citations within a cohort, so "top 1%" means top 1% of comparable work.

Cohort = FoS × publication year for papers, WIPO sector × grant year for patents.

Citation trend

p2p pat2p pat2pat appC

One row per (document, citing year): the full accrual history rather than a single total.

584M rows on the paper side alone. Every trajectory and convexity result is computed out-of-core from this table.

Convexity index

1 − mean(normalised citation age)

Shape of the min–max scaled cumulative citation curve. Below 0.5 = convex (late burst); above = concave (early peak, saturating).

Introduced by the validation notebooks rather than by a published paper — it is the quantity that makes "sleeping beauty" and "flash in the pan" comparable on one axis.

External agreement

in progress paper_validation §3c

The strongest validity evidence available: SciSciNet computes the same metrics on MAG, this pipeline computes them on OpenAlex 2026-01. Joining on the paper id and correlating says which metrics survive a change of citation graph. Only Spearman rank correlation is reported here — almost every downstream use depends on the ordering of papers, not on the absolute value, and rank agreement is the claim that actually transfers between two different citation graphs.

See the updated Agreement with SciSciNet figure (§3c) for metric-specific correlations, sample sizes and coverage from the current validation run, and §3b for the Uzzi 2×2 on SciSciNet's own scores. Earlier restricted-run atypicality results are shown separately in §9d. The Dimensions section (§14) compares the same metrics across the OpenAlex and Dimensions indices, and the cross-check compares the disruption kernel with an independent implementation.

Validation figures

118 figures · 9 notebooks

There is no gold standard for any of these metrics, so validation is face validity: each figure either reproduces a published result, or checks an invariant the build must satisfy. The 9 notebooks are kept apart below, because they are validated against different literature and different invariants; the last one cross-checks the disruption kernel against an independent implementation. Only figures exported by the validation notebooks are shown. Click any figure to enlarge it.

Papers

OpenAlex 20 figures · paper_validation.ipynb

Validated against the paper literature: the Funk & Owen-Smith disruption distribution, the Wu et al. team-size result, the Uzzi atypicality 2×2, and Ke et al. sleeping beauties — plus the convexity tests the notebooks add on top.

Papers — Dimensions

Dimensions 12 figures · dimension_validation.ipynb

The OpenAlex paper pipeline re-run, kernel for kernel, on the Dimensions June 2025 index: 153.8 M publications and 2.14 B reference edges. Fields are ANZSRC FoR 2020 divisions, a journal is source_titles.type = "journal", and the patent→paper channel is Dimensions' own patent index rather than Reliance on Science, so a difference from the OpenAlex figures is a property of the index, not of the metric. Section numbers follow case_law_validation so the families read side by side; a section whose Dimensions input has not been written yet is absent rather than empty.

Patents

PatentsView 34 figures · patent_validation.ipynb

No external gold standard exists for the patent metrics, so this is the invariant-heavy notebook: window nesting, the F + E + G = 1 identity, citation truncation by grant year, and the examiner / applicant and granted / pre-grant splits that only the patent side has.

Patent → paper

Reliance on Science 4 figures · pcs_validation.ipynb

How often US patents cite each paper. The checks here are about accrual and coverage: patent citations arrive an order of magnitude more slowly than paper citations, so the short windows are nearly empty by construction rather than by error.

Paper–patent pairs

PPP 12 figures · ppp_validation.ipynb

The 548,315-pair plus list links scientific and technological contributions. These validation figures join the document-level paper and patent citation histories, then apply figure-specific eligibility filters; the average cumulative trajectory in §5b covers 107,260 pairs. The separately inventoried PPP trend files are pair-level outputs. The hypothesis under test is that the paper side is convex and the patent side concave.

Case law

CASE 16 figures · case_law_validation.ipynb

5,179,698 US court opinions and 47,519,638 citations between them, 1800–2020. The same estimators as the paper and patent families — citation windows, the CD index and its F/E/G decomposition, the beauty coefficient — on a network whose citation culture is different: precedent is cited immediately and then, occasionally, rediscovered a century later.

Data provenance. The citation network and metadata were provided by the authors of Lee, S., Kim, T., Yoon, J., & Youn, H. (2024). When Common Law Ages: Two Centuries of Growing Inertia in US Judicial Opinions. arXiv preprint arXiv:2410.04493. Any use of the case_* outputs should cite it.

Author countries

OpenAlex 10 figures · author_country_validation.ipynb

Where the authors of 251.7 M OpenAlex works sit: ISO2 countries from the institution each affiliation resolved to, one row per work in paper_author_country.parquet. 44.6% of works have at least one located author. Coverage, country shares under full and fractional counting, international collaboration by team size, its relation to impact and disruption, partner pairs, country profiles, and a cross-check against the Dimensions author countries.

Inventor countries
